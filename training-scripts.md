The **Edge column** is the most important number on this page. It's the difference between what we think the fair line is and what the market is offering. A positive edge means the market is giving you better odds than our model thinks are justified.

**(Visual: Click into a specific oddsmaker detail view)**

This is where serious traders spend most of their time — comparing independent projections against real market data to find discrepancies.

**Pro tip:** Check the Oddsmaker early in the day before lines move. The biggest edges are often available when markets first open.

## Script 5: Closing Line Value (CLV)

**Target:** 2 minutes  
**Goal:** Explain CLV, why it matters, how to read it

---

**(Visual: Navigate to CLOSING LINE VALUES → MLB)**

Closing Line Value — or CLV — is the gold standard for measuring whether you have a real edge.

**(Visual: CLV page showing a list of games with CLV numbers)**

Here's the concept in plain English: if you bet a line at +110, and by the time the game starts that line has moved to -110, you got the better end of the deal. You have positive CLV.

**(Visual: Highlight a positive CLV entry in green)**

The CLV page shows you, for every game we projected, how our fair line compared to where the market **closed**. Green means we were on the right side — the market moved toward our number. Red means the market moved away.

**(Visual: Show the overall CLV record at the top)**

The summary at the top shows our aggregate CLV record. Over time, consistently positive CLV is the strongest statistical indicator of a profitable approach. It means you're not just getting lucky — you're getting better numbers than the market.

This is why we publish CLV publicly. Anyone can claim they have an edge. CLV is the proof.

## Script 6: Player Props Projections

**Target:** 2 minutes  
**Goal:** Show player props, grade A-D confidence system, finding value

---

**(Visual: Navigate to PLAYER PROPS PROJECTIONS → MLB)**

Player props are one of the fastest-growing markets, and they're often less efficient than game lines — meaning there's more opportunity.

**(Visual: Player props page showing a list of players and prop categories)**

Select a game and you'll see projections for key player stats — things like:

- Strikeouts for a pitcher
- Hits, RBIs, home runs for batters
- Any prop market the sportsbooks are offering

**(Visual: Show the grade column — A, B, C, D)**

Each projection comes with a **confidence grade**:

| Grade | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **A** | Highest confidence — strong signal across multiple models |
| **B** | Good confidence — clear edge, moderate variance |
| **C** | Moderate — edge exists but with higher uncertainty |
| **D** | Low confidence — weak signal, proceed with caution |

**(Visual: Click into a specific prop for detail)**

The fair line column shows what number our model projects. The market column shows what the sportsbook is offering. When there's a meaningful gap between the two — especially on an A or B grade — that's a prop worth investigating further.

Props are a great way to find edges the crowd misses because they require more work to price correctly. That's where AI has an advantage.

## Script 7: Simulation Engine

**Target:** 2 minutes  
**Goal:** Explain Monte Carlo simulations, how to read the outputs

---

**(Visual: Navigate to a specific game's detail page)**

Every game projection is backed by thousands of simulations.

**(Visual: Scroll to the simulation section)**

Here's how it works: our simulation engine runs each game **thousands of times**, factoring in everything — team strength, injuries, home field, rest days, bullpen usage for MLB, and hundreds of other variables.

**(Visual: Show simulation results — distribution chart)**

What you're seeing is the distribution of outcomes. For an MLB game:

- How often did each team win in the simulations?
- What's the most common final score?
- What's the spread cover percentage for each side?

**(Visual: Point to the cover percentages)**

If our simulations show the underdog covering the spread 62% of the time, and the sportsbook has them at +150, that's a mathematical edge. The simulations don't tell you what's going to happen — they tell you what's *likely* to happen, with probabilities based on data, not opinions.

Use simulations to confirm or challenge what you see in the projections and oddsmaker. When all three point in the same direction, the signal is strong.

## Script 8: Betting Splits

**Target:** 1.5 minutes  
**Goal:** Show sharp vs public money, how to combine with AI data

---

**(Visual: Navigate to a game, find the betting splits section)**

Betting splits show you where the money is going — and more importantly, *who* is betting it.

**(Visual: Betting splits interface)**

You'll see two key numbers for each game:

- **Public Betting %** — What percentage of tickets are on each side (the crowd)
- **Sharp Money %** — Where the big money / professional action is going

**(Visual: Highlight a split where public and sharp disagree)**

When the public is heavy on one side but the sharp money is on the other, that's a classic "fade the public" spot. But we don't just fade the public blindly — we combine this data with our AI projections.

When our fair line, the simulation engine, AND the sharp money are all pointing the same direction, you have a much stronger signal than any one of these alone.
